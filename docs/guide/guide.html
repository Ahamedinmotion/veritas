01 · The product

VERITAS in one page

Drop in a photo, a video or a voice clip. VERITAS runs independent forensic tests and gives one score, with its reasons.

Evidence
  • Photo
  • Video
  • Voice
15 independent tests
AI networks: ~70% of the weightAI-image, AI-face, face-swap (video) and AI-voice detectors. Pretrained, chosen by experiment.
Physics & signal testsELA error levels, sensor noise, FFT, JPEG tables, metadata, blink rate, face jitter, breaths.

Each test explains itself: an error map, a spectrum, a blink graph or a plain sentence.

Fusion

A weighted vote

Scores are added as log-odds, so a confident 95 counts far more than a lukewarm 60.

One verdict
0-100
  • below 35Authentic
  • 35-65Suspicious
  • above 65Synthetic

The honest headline numbers

Photos
91.3%
accuracy on 104 held-out images it never saw during tuning. AUC 0.971. 0 of 49 real photos flagged; 46 of 55 fakes caught (GAN faces 22 of 22).
Optimistic: 42 of the 49 real photos come from collections the network was trained on. Real life will be lower.
Voices
2 of 6
voice fakes caught in 10 outside clips (generators never used for tuning). All 4 of 4 real voices cleared.
Our weakest spot: new voice clones mostly get past it. Only 10 clips: a spot check.
Video face-swaps
0 12 of 15
fakes called synthetic, on 30 research clips from datasets neither network was trained on, after we added two published face-swap detectors. AUC 0.60 0.998.
Optimistic: the pair was chosen after seeing these clips (plausible range 0.87-1.00). 5 of 15 real clips now read suspicious; grainy webcam video: 11 of 39 real clips suspicious, 1 synthetic.

Spot the Fake: on the 30 game pictures VERITAS gets 27 right. People in a 2022 study sorting real faces from GAN faces were right 48.2% of the time: a coin toss.

What it cannot do

  • Prove anything. A score is evidence. A human makes the decision.
  • Catch most new voice clones. Only 2 of 6 outside fakes were caught.
  • Catch photo face-swaps made with a real person's face. Only 2 of 9 caught in our test.
  • Always clear real people. Grainy webcam video and compressed stills can read "suspicious" (5 of 12 real video stills flagged).
  • Vouch for new tools. Only 2019-2020 face-swap methods were tested; newer swap apps and deliberate attacks were not.
  • Claim we trained the big networks. They are published, pretrained models we chose by experiment, and we credit them.

01How does this fit "Intelligence Beyond Humans"?K

AI now makes faces people can't tell from real ones: in a 2022 study, people were right 48.2% of the time. VERITAS uses machine intelligence to see what eyes can't: frequency grids, sensor grain, blink statistics. And our own results show machines fail too, so the final call stays human.

Show: story chapter 3

02How do fifteen tests become one score?K

Each test gives a score from 0 to 100. We turn each one into log-odds and take a weighted vote, so a confident 95 counts far more than a lukewarm 60. The AI networks carry about 70% of the weight. Below 35 is authentic, 35 to 65 suspicious, above 65 synthetic.

HonestWe set the weights by hand. Only the calibration was fitted, and only on our calibration files.

Show: story 5, then five times

03Did you train the AI yourselves?K

No, and we say so on screen. A strong detector needs millions of images and a lot of GPU time. So we chose published, pretrained networks by experiment: 12 image models and 7 voice models. The best image model was also the smallest. We trained a small probe on top of the voice network. For face-swaps we benchmarked six published detectors on a test set we built and added two. Ours: the experiment, the physics tests, the fusion, the interface and the offline package.

Be exact about who did what. If you used AI coding assistants, tutorials or open-source code, say so plainly. Judges forgive help. They don't forgive over-claiming.

04How accurate is it? Will 91% hold up in real life?P

No, real life will be lower, and we say so. On 104 held-out images it never saw during tuning it scored 91.3% and flagged none of the 49 real photos. But with 104 images the true accuracy could be anywhere from 84 to 96%. Most of our real photos come from collections the network was trained on, so "no false alarms" is optimistic. And stills of real people from compressed videos were flagged 5 times out of 12.

Show: story 6

05What is AUC?P

Pick one fake and one real file at random: AUC is the chance that the fake gets the higher score. 0.5 is a coin toss, 1.0 is perfect; ours is 0.971 on photos. It judges the ranking, whatever cut-off you choose. Our voice test shows why that matters: AUC 0.92 on 10 outside clips, yet only 2 of 6 fakes crossed our cut-off.

06Aren't your demos cherry-picked?P

Yes, the evidence locker is chosen on purpose, like textbook examples, and the lab says so. That's why one exhibit is "A fake VERITAS misses". The real results are in chapter 6, on files never used for tuning, failures included. And you don't have to trust our samples: give us your own photo and we'll analyse it now.

Say this, never that

"It's 91% accurate.""On our held-out test it was 91.3%. Real life will be lower."
"It can detect any deepfake.""It catches AI-generated faces well, and most 2019-2020 face-swaps we tested. New voice clones get past it."
"This proves it's fake.""This is evidence. A human makes the decision."
"0.998: it's perfect.""On 30 clips from datasets neither network was trained on; the plausible range is 0.87 to 1.00."

04 · Questions 7-12 of 12

Judges' questions

Limits, attacks and privacy: say the weakness first.

07What if it calls a real person fake?P

That's the error we worry about most, because it can hurt someone. On held-out photos it flagged none of the 49 real images; the price is that it missed 9 of 55 fakes. Its weak spot is grainy real video: of 39 webcam-quality real clips, 11 came out suspicious and 1 synthetic. That's why 35 to 65 says "suspicious", not "fake", and why a score is evidence, never a verdict. A human decides.

08Does it catch video face-swaps?P

Better than before. We benchmarked six published detectors on 75 research clips and added two of them. On 30 clips from two datasets neither network was trained on, fakes called synthetic went from 0 to 12 of 15.

HonestThe AUC of 0.998 is optimistic: we chose the pair after seeing those clips, and with so few clips the plausible range is 0.87 to 1.00. Five of the 15 real clips now read suspicious, and only 2019-2020 methods were tested.

Show: story 6 (06·3)

09Why is voice your weakest result?P

Voice detectors learn shortcuts. Ours partly learned which microphone and dataset a clip came from, not what makes a voice fake. On 10 outside clips all 4 real voices passed, but only 2 of 6 fakes were caught. New voice clones mostly get past it, and we show that on screen.

Show: story 6 (06·2)

10Can it catch tomorrow's AI tools? Can someone fool it?K

Partly, and we can't prove it for tools that don't exist yet. Our image network learned from 4,803 generators, so it knows general generator textures, not one model's quirks. But a new design can leave new fingerprints, so it needs regular updates, like antivirus. And yes, a skilled attacker could fool it with adversarial noise. We haven't run that attack on VERITAS, so we give no number.

HonestThe "94 to 11" on the Limits slide is an illustration, not a measurement.

11What if the fake was sent on WhatsApp?K

Every re-share blurs the traces, so we tested it on all 104 held-out images. After WhatsApp-style compression it still caught 43 of 55 fakes and flagged none of the 49 real photos. The neural network survives compression; the classic physics tests suffer most. Screenshots we haven't tested on their own.

Show: story 7, the re-compress slider

12Where does my photo go?P

Nowhere. At the booth the engine runs on this laptop and needs no internet. It reads the file, analyses it and keeps no copy. Live Cam's face tracking runs inside the browser; only when you press Capture does one frame go to the engine, on this same laptop.

OnlineIf we are using the online version, the file is sent to our online engine for that analysis. Say so.

OnlineThe online version uploads nothing: it can only open saved reports. To analyse a visitor's own file, use this laptop.

Quick answers

Did you make deepfakes? Only test fakes, with one rule: no one famous and no one we know. Faces of people who don't exist, stock text-to-speech voices, test clones of two public-domain audiobook readers (never shipped), and one lip-sync of a paid, consenting actor from Google's research dataset.

Can a live deepfake pass the liveness check? Yes. The random order beats a photo or a recording, not a real-time face-swap. That's why it says "Challenge passed", not "Live human".

What does it cost? Software: nothing, it is all open-source. It runs on an ordinary laptop with no graphics card, after one download of about 3.5 GB.

Who would use it? People who check media: fact-checkers, newsrooms, school cyber-safety clubs, police cyber cells, bank fraud teams. It says "look closer, and here's why". It is a triage tool, not proof.

Six more months? Test today's swap apps, add Indian voices recorded on phones and WhatsApp, verify Content Credentials, attack our own detector, and get a bigger test collected by someone else.

Why 468 points in the story but 478 in the lab? The classic face mesh has 468 points; the newer tracker adds 10 iris points, 5 per eye.

Why "VERITAS"? It's Latin for "truth".

Open it a few minutes before you need it: a sleeping server can take a while to wake up. Online analysis can be slower than the laptop.
